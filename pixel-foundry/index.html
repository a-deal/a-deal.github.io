03 / How the work gets done

Close to the problem.
Hands on the work.

  1. 01

    Find the operating constraint.

    Start with the people doing the work. Map where time, money or usable information is lost. Agree on the problem, the baseline and the evidence that would justify further investment.

  2. 02

    Architect and deliver.

    Choose a bounded first implementation. Build it, integrate it with the existing workflow and put it in front of operators. Design for the next deployment as well as the immediate need.

  3. 03

    Turn field evidence into product decisions.

    Evaluate results against the agreed criteria. Trace failures, document limitations and use what the team learns to improve the software and prioritize the next investment.

05 / Working together

A few practical questions.

What does an engagement start with?

A conversation about the operation, the people involved and what needs to change. From there, we can scope a useful first piece of work: a prototype, a software integration or a product decision supported by customer evidence. Scope, deliverables and working arrangements are agreed before work begins.

Do you work on new products or existing systems?

Both. The work can start with a new prototype or with an existing application, data pipeline or operator workflow. The first step is understanding what is already in place and what a useful improvement would require.

Is this engineering, product strategy or both?

Hands-on software engineering with product judgment. I can help clarify what is worth building, implement the software and work with the team to evaluate the result. Product strategy is also available as a focused engagement when the main question is what to build next.
